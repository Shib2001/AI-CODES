# Now there are two ways to make a voice agent 

1. S2S : speech to speech , where a user speech query is directly feeded into the model 
and what do we get get back in return a speech response , that's called a S2S architecture. 

PROS :
- Low latency: audio can be processed and answered directly without waiting for a separate transcription and speech-generation pipeline.
- More natural conversations: the model can respond with expressive speech, tone, and conversational timing.
- Audio understanding: depending on the model, it can use vocal cues such as tone and emotion that may not appear in a text transcript.
- Simpler pipeline: fewer separate components need to be connected and maintained.

CONS :
- Harder to inspect and debug: the input and output are audio, so it may be less obvious why the model responded a certain way.
- Less control over individual stages: it can be harder to customize transcription, text processing, or speech generation separately.
- Model and provider constraints: available voices, languages, audio features, and integrations depend on the chosen model.
- Testing and accessibility may need extra work, such as saving transcripts or providing text alternatives.

2. Chained : 

( Speech to text )
The user's audio is sent to a speech-to-text (STT) model or service.
It recognizes the spoken words and returns a text transcript, like subtitles or transcription.
That transcript is then sent to the LLM as the user's message.
Examples of STT tools include Whisper and cloud speech-recognition services.


( Text to Text )
The LLM reads the transcript, understands the request, and generates a text response.


( Text to speech )

The LLM's text response is sent to a text-to-speech (TTS) model or service.
TTS generates audio that speaks the response aloud; the voice, language, and speaking style may be configurable.
Examples of TTS tools include OpenAI text-to-speech, ElevenLabs, and cloud speech-synthesis services.



PROS :
- Easier to inspect and debug: the transcript and generated text make it clear what the system heard and what it plans to say.
- More control: each stage can be selected, configured, or replaced independently (for example, choosing a different speech-to-text or text-to-speech service).
- Easier to add text-based steps such as moderation, prompt logic, tools, or business rules between transcription and the LLM.
- Transcripts are naturally available for captions, searchable logs, and accessibility features.
- Here i can use gemini , claude , gpt or any model just for text to text 

CONS :
- Higher latency: audio must pass through transcription, the LLM, and speech generation, often one after another.
- Errors can accumulate: a speech-to-text mistake can cause the LLM to misunderstand the request, and speech generation can introduce further issues.
- More components to build and maintain: each stage needs configuration, monitoring, and reliable integration with the others.
- Text conversion can lose vocal cues such as emotion, emphasis, or background-audio context.
